## Objections and competitors

- *Also seen (weaker evidence, speculative):* My kit order keeps showing up with things missing or wrong. [OBJ-01]; The kit vegetables sometimes arrive not fresh or rotten. [OBJ-02]; Kit portions are too small for what I pay. [OBJ-03]; Meal prep takes too much of my week, and it stresses me out. [OBJ-04]; I like the meals, but I don't want to be forced into a subscription or have to fight to cancel. [OBJ-05]

**How to answer them:**
- OBJ-01: Fair, a box with things missing or wrong is annoying. From what people say, it's a logistics problem, not a food one. If you want to skip the risk, a week from staples you already have (rice, pasta, eggs, canned beans) is a no-delivery backup.
- OBJ-02: Rotten veg in a box you paid for is not it. It's an observation from a few posts, not a rule for every kit. If you're prepping yourself, prep the parts and freeze what you won't get to, so nothing goes off before you use it.
- OBJ-03: People say the food is good, but small portions for the price don't add up. Put the cost per meal next to a week you cook yourself and see if it works for you.
- OBJ-04: Prep shouldn't eat your whole week. Prep components, not whole meals, cook a double batch and freeze half. Some people log 4-5 hours, so keep it to what you'll actually eat and skip the rest.
- OBJ-05: Liking the meals but not wanting a subscription you have to fight to cancel is a real concern. Check the cancellation terms before you order, or keep a cheap DIY week as the fallback.

### Creative brief
- **Objective:** Help budget-focused Gen Z cooks feel understood and give them copyable, low-effort ways to prep. The aim is to earn trust and replies in the communities where they swap tips, not to sell.
- **Audience:** Gen Z adults, 18-27, students and early-career, cooking for themselves on a tight budget. They say things like 'Sunday prep day', 'frozen anything', 'decision fatigue' and 'macro-friendly'. Many are cooking for one. A few judge meal kits on portions, price and freshness.
- **Insight:** They want to save money and not think about food every day, but prep can eat hours, reheating can be slow, and eating the same thing all week is a turn-off. Reading the evidence as a set of observations, they want the savings without the grind.
- **Message:** Cheap, flexible prep that doesn't take over your week: staples you already have, freeze half, prep the parts, reheat fast.
- **Tone:** Plain, practical and peer-to-peer. Dry humour about being broke, casual spelling, the occasional emoji. In review contexts, evaluative: praise, then the honest catch.
- **Mandatories:** State a real total cost or the number of meals when you talk about budget; Give the actual list or components, kept short; Frame findings as observations from the research, not facts; Cover at least one of: freeze half and reheat later, prep components, quick make-ahead breakfast, macro-friendly
- **Avoid:** Recipes with long ingredient lists; Suggesting everyone needs a full weekend of prep; Premium or gourmet framing; Assuming everyone likes leftovers; Invented prices, savings or health claims
