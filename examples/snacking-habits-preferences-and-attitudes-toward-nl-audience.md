### Creative brief
- **Objective:** Launch a snack brand in the Netherlands by earning trust and first trial among everyday sweet and savoury snackers aged 18-40, using community conversation and reviewer-led discovery rather than vague health claims.
- **Audience:** Dutch snackers (18-40) who love a specific flavour ('Ik hou van chips!'), check the label themselves, let reviewers taste new supermarket products first, and still miss the snack that got pulled. Some are health-conscious and want real protein or less sugar, but they will not give up taste.
- **Insight:** People want to snack a bit better, but they have been burned: 'healthy' claims don't hold up, 'minder zout' can mean 'weinig smaak', and favourites disappear without explanation. So they trust their own label check, other people's tasting, and their own memory of what really tasted good. (Observations from the pack, not proven facts.)
- **Message:** Lekker first, and you can check it yourself: real flavour, real numbers, easy to find in the supermarket.
- **Tone:** Casual and direct, Dutch with English flavour names. Humorous and honest, no polished ad language. Platform-adapted: blunt on forums, warm on YouTube, quick and sceptical on TikTok.
- **Mandatories:** Name the exact flavour and brand/product; Show real numbers (kcal, sugar, protein and fibre where relevant) and a short, checkable ingredient list; Mention taste ('lekker') whenever a health or 'minder' change is mentioned; Say where to buy it; Use audience words naturally (e.g. verslavend lekker, jeugdsentiment, zoutje)
- **Avoid:** Vague 'healthy' claims without kcal, sugar or ingredient detail; Pushing 'less salt' without mentioning taste; Positioning bami or nasi as the safe crowd-pleaser on a platter; Overly polished or formal ad language; Assuming the snackbar is a premium place; Presenting observations from the pack as proven facts
