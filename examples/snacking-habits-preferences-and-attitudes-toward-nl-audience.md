### Creative brief
- **Objective:** Launch a new snack brand in the Netherlands by earning trial among 18-40 snackers who buy everyday sweet and savoury snacks, starting with credibility in the places where they already judge snacks (forums, Reddit, YouTube tests, TikTok).
- **Audience:** Dutch snackers 18-40 who pick chips, bar snacks and sweet snacks on taste, crunch and price, check kcal, sugar and the ingredient list, follow supermarket test videos, and miss snacks that vanished or that Dutch shops don't stock. Their words: lekker, verslavend lekker, jeugdsentiment, gatverdamme, zoutje.
- **Insight:** Dutch snackers want a snack that is better for them, but they don't take anyone's word for it: they check the kcal and sugar numbers themselves, and a snack that tastes worse than the familiar one is dismissed. (Observation from the research pack, not a proven fact.)
- **Message:** A new snack you can check for yourself: real numbers, proper flavour and crunch, and a price you can compare.
- **Tone:** Casual, direct and often funny. Short verdicts, a bit of emoji, numbers instead of adjectives. Dutch first, English product and flavour names as the audience uses them.
- **Mandatories:** Name the exact flavour or item (paprika, bolognese, cheese onion, kaassoufflé); Show real kcal and sugar per 100 g whenever a health point is made; Mention taste together with crunch, and a clear price comparison; Use 'lekker' and nostalgic framing where it fits (jeugdsentiment); Only claim protein, fibre or digestion benefits the product itself supports
- **Avoid:** Vague 'healthy' claims without numbers or ingredients; Calling a snack healthy while it has visible high kcal or sugar; Reformulating (e.g. less salt) without protecting the taste; Treating all snack-bar items as universally loved: bamischijf, eierbal and kaassoufflé divide opinion; Corporate or stiff language

### Compliance flags (check with legal - not legal advice)
- CMP-01 on HOOK-02 (food_nutrition): Calling a snack 'addictive' trivialises addiction and can imply overconsumption of a high-fat/salt food. Safer: Paprika, bolognese of cheese onion: kies je kant. Welke chipsmaak vind jij onweerstaanbaar lekker?
- CMP-02 on HOOK-11 (other): A named-competitor price comparison must be verifiable, current and like-for-like, and 'taste the difference' implies superiority. Safer: Smaak, crunch én prijs naast elkaar: wat vind jij van de verschillen? (Alleen met actuele, controleerbare prijzen op een vermelde datum.)
- CMP-03 on HOOK-12 (food_nutrition): Highlighting protein and fibre may be read as a nutrition claim ('source of'/'high in') that is only allowed when thresholds are met. Safer: Eiwit en vezels staan in de voedingswaarde-tabel: hoeveel zit er per 100 g in? Wij noemen het getal, zonder er een claim van te maken.
- CMP-04 on DO-02 (other): Own test video comparing your snack with named competitor products may be misleading or denigrating, and is not independent. Safer: Make a transparent, dated tasting of snacks with a clear disclosure that it is your own product, objective criteria and no claims of superiority.
- CMP-05 on PLN-03 (other): A brand-run comparison with competitors and seeded creator tests risk misleading comparison and undisclosed sponsorship. Safer: Offer samples to creators with a clear #ad/sponsored disclosure, and keep your own tasting factual, dated and free of superiority claims.
- CMP-06 on OPP-01 (food_nutrition): Leading with protein/fibre for gym and health-minded audiences implies 'high protein/source of fibre' or health benefits that need qualifying levels. Safer: Show the protein and fibre per 100 g factually from the label; only use 'bron van eiwit/vezels' if the legal thresholds are met.
- CMP-07 on OPP-03 (health_supplements): 'Gentle on the stomach' or bloating relief is an unauthorised health claim implying a digestive benefit. Safer: Avoid digestion promises; describe the ingredients factually, e.g. 'gemaakt met een korte ingrediëntenlijst'.
- CMP-08 on OPP-04 (food_nutrition): 'Healthier' is a vague comparative health claim and 'affordable' needs substantiation. Safer: Snacks with X g less sugar than our regular version (if ≥30% less), at a price of €X.
- CMP-09 on OPP-05 (food_nutrition): 'Less sugar' is a comparative nutrition claim needing ≥30% reduction versus a stated reference; 'fewer additives' implies a health benefit. Safer: Show the full ingredient list clearly; say 'bevat X g suiker per 100 g' or 'minder suiker dan [product]' only with a verified reduction.

## Guardrails

- **Never claim:** verslavend lekker; 4 cent goedkoper bij Aldi; Eiwit en vezels op de zak; your snack next to familiar chips; test of familiar chips next to yours; Lead with actual protein and fibre amounts; gentle-on-the-stomach angle; Affordable healthier snack range; less sugar and fewer additives
- **Sensitivities:** Health positioning invites push-back. Posts check calories and sugar and question the word 'gezond', so unproven claims could draw sceptical replies.; Reformulation such as less salt can split fans. One post prefers the lower-salt chips, another is annoyed by a 'minder zout' trend, so test with loyal buyers.; Protein or health bars face open taste scorn in comments, even when a reviewer praises them. Credibility depends on honest taste claims.; Nostalgia is emotional. Removing or replacing a loved product can draw angry posts, and a stand-in is judged against the original.; The evidence on many topics is thin, often one or two posts, and mostly comes from forums and video comments. Do not treat it as a full picture of Dutch snackers.
- **Quotes:** Real people's words are for insight and briefs, not for ads without permission.
