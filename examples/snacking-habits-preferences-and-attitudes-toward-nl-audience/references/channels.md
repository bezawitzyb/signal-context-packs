# Channels

1. web_forum: Biggest evidence base (141 posts). It is where the audience gives blunt verdicts on missing snacks, flavours, prices and 'healthy' claims, and it has the platter and regional-pride jokes. The other platforms are supported by smaller volumes, and any platform not listed here (e.g. Instagram, Reddit) is a test. Formats: Question threads, Flavour polls (paprika vs bolognese vs plain vs cheese), Replies with label facts and price. Where: Dutch food and snack forum threads on new supermarket products, Threads on snacks pulled from the shelf, Borrel and snackbar discussion threads. Tone: Blunt, joking, short verdicts, mock outrage and emoticons. Plain Dutch, no corporate language. (CHN-01)
2. youtube: Friendly, curious audience (47 posts) with local pride in the comments. Supermarket-test videos are how they find out which new products are worth trying. This is a smaller evidence base than the forum. Formats: Supermarket-test pitches and sample seeding, Comment replies about regional specialties, Short taste-and-crunch review. Where: PROEFWERK-style supermarket test videos, Comment sections of Dutch food and snack videos. Tone: Friendly and curious, with emoji and room for regional pride. Say 'nieuwe producten uit de supermarkt' and 'proberen'. (CHN-02)
3. tiktok: Small base (22 posts) but a clear style: sceptical and quick, with calorie numbers and jokes. It suits label-reading and taste-and-crunch formats. Treat it as a test, since the evidence on discovery via TikTok is inconclusive. Formats: Label read-out with kcal, sugar and price, 10-second crunch test, Flavour showdown. Where: #snacktest, #proefwerk, #nieuwindeschappen, #chipssmaak. Tone: Quick, sceptical and number-driven, with jokes and emoji. Show the numbers first, then the verdict. (CHN-03)

## How each platform sounds
- web_forum (141 posts): Blunt, joking and opinionated: short verdicts, mock-outrage and emoticons. Detailed chip and snack-bar talk: specific flavours and textures, ranking snacks, price differences between shops, and missed discontinued snacks.
- youtube (47 posts): Friendly and curious, with emoji, comments on videos and local pride. Supermarket-test videos and regional-food comments. The talk is about discovering new products and recommending local specialties.
- tiktok (22 posts): Sceptical and quick, with calorie numbers, jokes and emoji. Health claims get tested here: viewers answer with kcal and sugar figures and say the label doesn't make a snack healthy. Price pushback also appears.
