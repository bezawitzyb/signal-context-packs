# Channels

1. reddit: This is where the evidence is strongest (39 posts). The tone is practical and tip-swapping, and the posts that perform are weekly student preps with a total cost, staples methods, and beginner questions. Formats: Weekly prep post with total cost and a component list, Short reply with two concrete make-ahead ideas, Either/or question with a budget limit, Numbered prep-day log, Macros-per-serving recipe post. Where: Budget and college meal prep communities, Weekly meal prep log threads, Cooking-for-one and beginner cooking communities, Fitness meal prep communities (macros per serving). Tone: Peer-to-peer and plain. Dry humour about being broke is fine. Give numbers (cost, time, servings) and the actual list. No polish or selling. (CHN-01)
2. web_review: This is the second-largest source (26 posts). People praise taste and then complain about value, portions, missing items, freshness and subscriptions. The pack shows this mainly for meal kits, so it is a place to listen and answer, not to broadcast. Formats: Balanced first-person review-style posts (praise first, then the honest catch), Cost-per-week comparisons against DIY prep, Replies to kit complaints with a cheap staples alternative. Where: Meal kit review pages and consumer review sites, Reviews of Hello Fresh and similar services. Tone: More evaluative: say what's good, then what lets it down. Be concrete about price and portions, and avoid hype. (CHN-02)
3. web_editorial: Budget Bytes-style recipe pages are cited by the audience as a place to find recipes to double and freeze. A cheat sheet or weekly budget menu fits this format. Only Reddit and web reviews have deep evidence, so this channel is a test and TikTok, YouTube and Instagram are not covered by the pack. Formats: Freeze-and-reheat cheat sheet, Weekly budget menu with a stated total cost, Microwave vs air fryer decision guide. Where: Budget recipe sites and blogs, Search results for 'freezer meals' and 'budget meal prep'. Tone: Quick-reference and no-fuss. Short, scannable, with numbers and no long ingredient lists. (CHN-03)

## How each platform sounds
- reddit (39 posts): Practical, peer-to-peer and tip-swapping: recipes, reheating times, questions and weekly prep logs. Meal prep is framed as DIY problem-solving. People freeze batches, build meals from cheap pantry staples, and post budgeted weekly preps.
- web_review (26 posts): Evaluative and mixed: praise for taste, then complaints about value. Meal prep appears as meal kits judged on portion size, price, missing items and freshness rather than on DIY technique.
