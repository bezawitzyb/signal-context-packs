# Channels

1. reddit: Reddit is where most of the evidence sits (39 posts). The tone is peer-to-peer, with how-to questions, weekly logs with costs, and recipe swaps. Formats with a stated cost, a narrow question, or a concrete swap perform. Evidence comes from Reddit and web reviews only, so TikTok, Instagram and YouTube are tests, not priorities. Formats: Weekly numbered prep log with total cost, Component-by-component lunch breakdown, Helpful comment answering a narrow question (freezing, reheating, breakfast swaps), Honest beginner 'what should I stock?' style question. Where: Meal prep communities, Budget and cheap-cooking communities, College student cooking communities. Tone: Casual, practical, frank about money. Lowercase asides, the odd emoji, mild self-deprecating humour about being broke or tired. Give the price, the steps and what went wrong. (CHN-01)
2. web_review: Meal kit talk is concentrated in web reviews (26 posts). The pattern is praise for taste, then complaints about portions, price, missing items, spoiled produce and subscriptions. This is a place to listen and to position cheap DIY batch cooking as an alternative. Treat it as an observation, not a proven preference. Formats: Balanced comparison of a meal kit vs cooking staples, covering portions and cost, Reply-style content that addresses portion size head-on. Where: Public meal kit review pages, Hello Fresh review threads. Tone: Evaluative and mixed. Admit what is good (taste, ease) before the letdowns, and never promise kit value without addressing portion size. (CHN-02)
3. web_editorial: Budget Bytes is cited on Reddit as a source for freezer-friendly prep, which suggests people go to recipe sites for guides. Search-style how-tos on freezing and reheating, and cheap week-of-dinners plans, fit this channel. The evidence is indirect, so treat it as a test. Formats: Freeze-and-reheat how-to, Week-of-dinners plan with a stated budget, Make-ahead breakfast guide. Where: Budget recipe sites, Search queries on freezing and reheating cooked dishes. Tone: Plain how-to language. Short steps, the price up front, staples people already have. (CHN-03)

## How each platform sounds
- reddit (39 posts): Practical and peer-to-peer: people ask how-to questions, share weekly prep logs with costs, and swap recipes. Reddit focuses on DIY methods: freezing and reheating, pantry-staple recipes, and weekly prep posts with prices.
- web_review (26 posts): Evaluative and mixed: praise for taste, then complaints. Reviews judge meal kits on portion size, price after offers end, missing items and freshness, not on DIY method.
