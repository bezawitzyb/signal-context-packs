# Channels

1. tiktok: Largest and most distinctive evidence: dorm and rice-cooker recipes, numbered series, budget questions and save-this culture all sit here. Formats: short_video, numbered series, 'meal prep with me'. Where: #dormdinners, #dormlife, #GymTok, #nobakecon, #lazygirlmealprep. Tone: Upbeat and short, light self-deprecating humour, emoji, a save-this prompt. (CHN-01)
2. reddit: Second biggest source: practical, detail-led advice, weekly prep logs and short usable answers to breakfast and storage questions. Formats: text_post, weekly numbered log, helpful replies. Where: meal prep communities, budget cooking communities, college and dorm communities. Tone: Plainer, advice-led, state the constraints (time, budget, equipment) up front. (CHN-02)
3. youtube: A concrete price, time frame and meal count in the title performed here, but it is a single data point, so treat YouTube as a test. Formats: short_video, one-week budget video. Where: college budget meal prep. Tone: Practical and specific; put the cost and the time frame in the title. (CHN-03)
4. x: One provocative-question post drew replies, so use X as a test for opinion-starting questions only; evidence is thin. Formats: text_post, question. Where: -. Tone: Short, curious, a bit cheeky; invite opinions. (CHN-04)

## How each platform sounds
- reddit (84 posts): Practical, advice-swapping and detailed, with questions and weekly prep logs. Storage, freezing and food-safety questions show up here, along with ingredient prep and week-by-week logs.
- tiktok (80 posts): Upbeat and short, with emoji, hashtags and save-this prompts. Budget, dorm microwave meals, high protein and lazy-girl framing show up here, often as creator videos teaching college students.
