# Snacking habits and attitudes toward new snack brands in the Netherlands - NL audience context

Who: Dutch snack buyers aged roughly 18-40 who snack between meals, at work/study, and socially, including health-conscious and indulgent snackers. Market NL, languages nl, en.
71 public posts from about 63 people, mostly on YouTube and TikTok. This is what vocal people say online, not a survey of the whole market. Early signals: fewer posts than a full pack needs, so treat findings as signals to test. Research from 10 Oct 2026.

Position: Lekker, maar nét wat slimmer snacken. For: Nederlandse snackers van 18-40 die tussendoortjes willen die lekker blijven, langer vullen en niet voelen als een compromis. Ze kijken eerst naar taste tests en hauls voordat ze iets nieuws proberen.
Check with legal first: uses wording the legal check flagged (nét wat slimmer). Get sign-off before using it publicly.

Goal (positioning): The evidence points to one gap to own: a snack that tastes like a treat and shows, in checkable terms, why it is a smarter pick (observation, not proven fact).
Goal (content plan): The audience discovers products through taste tests, hauls and short YouTube and TikTok formats, so the launch content should live inside those formats rather than in brand ads (the evidence is moderate, and HYP-04 is inconclusive).

## Do first
- Put sugar and protein figures on the front of the pack and publish one short explainer (Instagram carousel plus a TikTok) titled along the lines of 'Waarom noemen we dit nét wat slimmer?', with the real numbers from your own recipe. (DO-01)
  Check with legal first: Explaining 'slimmer' through sugar/protein figures needs a clear reference and valid nutrition claims; the term is an implied health claim. Safer: Wat zit er in onze snack? Bekijk de cijfers voor suiker en eiwit per portie.
- Post a TikTok 'YAY of NAY' with 2-3 flavour or crunch options of the new snack, asking 'Wil jij een lekkere, knapperige eiwitboost die nét wat slimmer is?', and ask people to answer in the comments. (DO-02)
  Check with legal first: Protein and 'slimmer' claims appear in the call to action without substantiation. Safer: Wil jij een lekkere, knapperige snack met [x g eiwit per portie]? YAY of NAY?
- Send launch samples to 3-5 public YouTube and TikTok creators who do supermarket taste tests and snack hauls, with a new-flavour brief and a note to give an honest verdict, including how it compares to what they already buy. (DO-03)
  Check with legal first: Free samples to creators require clear ad disclosure, and comparisons with other products must be fair and not imply unsubstantiated superiority. Safer: Send samples with a note to label content as reclame/#ad, give an honest opinion, and avoid health or superiority claims about other products.

## What we heard (strength in brackets; only strong may be stated as fact)
- The Lidl explosion protein bar contains beef collagen, which is a shame. (emerging, 2 of 71 posts; OBJ-03)
- Nice packaging and a new flavour make me want to try it. (emerging, 3 of 71 posts; THM-05)
- Protein bars mostly let me down. (emerging, 2 of 71 posts; PAIN-02)
- Dutch snacks from childhood still mean something to me. (speculative, 2 of 71 posts; THM-08)
- After sweet snacks I got a big energy dip. (speculative, 2 of 71 posts; PAIN-01)

## Voice
Tone: Casual, warm and enthusiastic, with plenty of emoji, exclamation marks and hashtags on social posts; web reviewers sound more worried and practical.
Code-switching: Dutch with English terms such as snack haul, taste test, unboxing, viral and foodie, and some posts written fully in English.
Their words: snacktip = a quick recommendation for a snack; tussendoortjes = between-meal snacks; lekker = tasty, nice; roze koeken = pink-iced cookies, a nostalgic Dutch treat; snoep = sweets, candy; snack haul = video showing purchased snacks; plopkoeken = puffy pastry-like cookies; spotgoedkoop = dirt cheap; team Hüttenkäse = fan of cottage cheese; stropfwaffel = syrup waffle; bakleverworst = Twents liver sausage specialty

## Guardrails
Say: lekker; snacktip; nieuwe producten testen
Not: clinical diet or calorie-counting language as the main hook; telling people that junk in a snack haul is wrong; calling healthy snacks luxury or premium without addressing the price
Don't claim without a legal check: een snack nét wat slimmer; energiedip na iets zoets; eiwitsnack die écht wat kan; eiwitboost die nét wat slimmer is; naast jullie favoriete tussendoortjes; Waarom heet die reep 'gezond'?; Snel eiwit na het sporten; zak leeg zonder dat je het doorhad; Spotgoedkoop of gewoon lekker?; waarom noemen we dit nét wat slimmer
Sensitive: Calling a snack 'healthy' invites scrutiny: one reader questions sugar in bars listed as healthy, and another says some products try to look healthy with bad ingredients. Unclear sugar claims could backfire.; Price is a sore point: healthy snacks are called expensive, and one poster on a tight budget asks for cheaper ones. A premium-priced launch may be dismissed.; Ingredient surprises can sour trust: a protein bar with beef collagen drew a regretful comment. Unclear labelling of animal-based or diet-relevant ingredients is a risk.; Talk about sweets and weight is sensitive: one poster calls choosing between getting fat and eating chocolate a terrible decision. Guilt or diet-shaming copy could misfire.; Comparisons with Albert Heijn and Lidl are made in public. Against supermarket own-label products a new brand may be judged as just another copy or as lower quality.; Several findings rest on creator and brand posts, not audience comments, and some clusters have only one or two posts. Treat the motivations as directional.
