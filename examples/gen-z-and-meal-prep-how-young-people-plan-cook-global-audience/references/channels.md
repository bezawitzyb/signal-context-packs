# Channels

1. tiktok: The pack's richest creative evidence is here: weekly reset videos, budget-labelled videos, recipe-request comments and posts with numbers in the headline. Evidence comes mainly from TikTok and Reddit, so treat other platforms as tests. Formats: short_video with recipe in the caption, weekly reset video, day-four rescue video. Where: #sundayreset, #highproteinmeals, #lazygirlmealprep, #collegestudent, #easymealprep. Tone: Upbeat and casual, emoji, short captions, light self-deprecating humour ('cba', 'terrible at cooking'). (CHN-01)
2. reddit: Largest post count in the pack, with advice-seeking questions on cost, storage and food going to waste, plus long first-person budget stories. It is where practical feasibility seems to get checked. Formats: plain-text show-and-tell 'this past week' post, question thread, first-person budget method story. Where: Public meal prep and budget cooking subreddits. Tone: Plain, direct and advice-seeking. No emoji spam, no hard sell; share the real cost and the real mess. (CHN-02)
3. youtube: One reflective post with an open question to the audience performed well, and YouTube comments are a possible place to check feasibility. Evidence is thin here, so this is a test. Formats: short reflective lesson ending in a question, freezer-meal how-to. Where: Comment questions: what do you keep ready in the fridge or freezer?. Tone: Reflective and practical, ending with an open question. (CHN-03)
4. instagram: Only one playful food post is in the pack; its appeal seems to come from tone and the food. Test it by repurposing TikTok clips; do not expect it to lead. Formats: reel repurposed from TikTok, carousel of the week's prep. Where: #sundayreset, #mealinspo. Tone: Witty, rhythmic captions, food-first. (CHN-04)

## How each platform sounds
- reddit (115 posts): Practical and advice-seeking, with detailed questions and tips, mostly in plain text. Reddit posts get into the logistics: fridge and freezer space, cooling and dating food, how long to prep for, and sessions that feel too big.
- tiktok (91 posts): Upbeat and casual, with emoji, hashtags and short captions. TikTok posts frame meal prep as quick, lazy or high-protein content, weekend resets and recipe requests in comments, with jokes about food being eaten.
