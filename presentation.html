    • How it collects

    The agent chooses. The tools enforce.

    A model is good at deciding where to look next, and bad at counting and at not inventing quotes. So the agent picks the sources, and the tools hold the data and enforce every limit. It never sees a raw post and never writes a number.

    InputBriefOne line from the marketer
    AI · SonnetQuestionsUp to 4; goal and offer always covered
    AI + codeEditable planHypotheses, questions, 3–6 starting sources
    AI · SonnetAgent loop14 tools, a reason per call, ≤ 3 at once
    Code + HaikuClean in every toolBefore anything returns
    CodePostgresEvery stage saved; posts expire in 30 days
    Source selection

    14 tools: 8 for 7 social platforms, web search and fetch, forum feeds, Trends, a coverage report and finish.

    Markets decide languages. English always joins; a country the brief names keeps its language. No platform gets more than half of the starting plan.

    Explore small, then exploit. A new search starts at 20 posts (40 Standard) and gets full size once 30% of it proves relevant.

    Keyword search only, no account of ours. Never a person or profile. Every platform has a backup actor; both fail → a blind spot.

    Fall back, never repeat. Crash → untried starting sources. Thin evidence → top up from kept ones. A dropped source is never called again.

    Data handling, inside every tool
    1. Hash the author name with a salt, in the same function that receives it
    2. Redact emails, phones, handles and names; strip handles from post links
    3. Parse dates in 12 languages; drop posts outside the window (180 days)
    4. De-duplicate with MinHash (0.8), keeping the more engaged copy
    5. Filter spam: link-only, hashtag floods, promos, repeated templates
    6. Haiku relevance check, also tagging language and market
    7. Engagement percentile within each platform
    The agent sees only: collected · kept · relevant % · dropped and why · new terms · 3 samples · budget left

    Scraped text reaches a model only inside <untrusted_user_content>.

    "Safe to state" is earned.

    Models read and write. Code counts, scores and checks every quote and claim, and only then lets a finding be stated as fact.

    SIGNAL · Context Packs03 / 04 · From posts to a claim
  • "This is the part the demo can't show you: how a scraped post becomes something the tool will call a fact."
  • Walk the gates left to right: pink = a model step, yellow = a code step. The model never outputs a count (metrics.py); quotes are checked as exact substrings (verify.py); safe_to_assert = strong + observed + supported (confidence.py).
  • Step 04: the generic answer is written with no evidence. Findings it already covers aren't marked "non-obvious".
  • Entailment is checked by Opus, a stronger model than the writer: 137 of 150 sampled claims.
  • "Even a strong finding isn't safe to state if it's our interpretation. The student-budget theme on slide 1 scores 0.82 and is strong, but it's inferred, so it isn't marked safe."
  • The downstream test (scripts/demo_agent): with the pack, the scripts used 8 audience terms and cited 16 pack items, but still stated 4 facts not marked safe. Say it plainly: rules in a prompt aren't enough, which is why the pipeline enforces everything in code.
  • If asked "isn't 100% quotes trivially true?": yes, that's the point. It's a guarantee, not a score, so it isn't counted as an eval result.
  • "Why not RAG or embeddings?" Clustering with a membership check plus exact-substring quotes keeps every claim traceable. Local ML models also don't fit a free 512 MB server (peak 201 MB).

    Session notes · B2B SaaS marketer

    Honest gaps
    • Relevance is below 60% on 3 of 5 briefs (34–80%). Off-topic posts never become evidence, but they cost collection time.
    • Quick runs often run out of their $1.40 analysis budget and end as evidence-only packs.
    • Speed: 14–17 min in the latest Quick runs, against a 7-minute target. (The 69–102 min on /evals includes analysis I finished later with extra budget.)
    • Forum feeds (V13) are not yet proven in a full run.
    • Not re-tested with users since the 13 changes shipped.
    Next
    • Product: sharper probe queries, rebalanced Quick budgets, more B2B buyer voices.
    • Customer to test: freelancers covering many industries, small teams, or white-label for agencies.
    • Format: a one-off pack, or an ongoing feed into the content calendar.
    • Price: above a $1.90–5.67 run cost.
    • Position vs HubSpot, SEMrush, Supermetrics: quote-backed audience voice, local and multilingual.
