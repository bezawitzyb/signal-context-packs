WEB SCRAPING USE CASES FOR MARKETING AND CONTENT TEAMS
=======================================================

This document combines a general overview of how marketing and content teams use
web scraping with the use cases that came up in four user interviews. Each use case
appears once. Use cases that interviewees raised are marked [Interview: #], where:

  1 = Freelance marketer/contractor (digital manufacturing clients)
  2 = Lifecycle marketer at Booking.com for Business
  3 = Marketer at a Microsoft-ecosystem company (AI agents)
  4 = B2B SaaS marketer building an event-venue product (Amsterdam)


1. AUDIENCE AND MARKET RESEARCH
-------------------------------
- Audience language and terminology: collect the exact words, jargon and phrasing the
  audience uses so copy and content speak their language. [Interview: 1, 3, 4]
- Pain points, objections and worries: find what frustrates the audience and what stops
  them from buying or adopting (e.g. unclear pricing, cost visibility, governance).
  [Interview: 1, 3, 4]
- Audience segmentation: distinguish how buyers, influencers and end users each talk
  about a product, so messaging can be tailored to each. [Interview: 1]
- Customer journey mapping: understand the steps people take before buying, such as how
  they search, compare and contact providers. [Interview: 4]
- Assumption and hypothesis validation: check your own beliefs about the market against
  real conversations, with direct quotes as evidence. [Interview: 4]
- Product and business idea validation: test whether there is real demand or an
  unserved need ("white space") before building. [Interview: 1, 4]
- Local and cultural nuance: learn how a specific country or region communicates
  (language, tone, formality, habits) to market there as a non-native.
  [Interview: 1, 3, 4]
- Persona development: refine buyer personas using community discussions, job titles
  and public profiles.
- Market sizing: count players, products or listings in a category.
- Product and category trends: track which products, features or categories are rising.
- Rapid industry onboarding: give contractors and agencies a fast primer on an
  unfamiliar industry, like a personalized analyst report. [Interview: 1]


2. CONTENT STRATEGY AND CREATION
--------------------------------
- Topic and trend discovery: mine forums, Q&A sites, Reddit, news and social platforms
  for what the audience is asking and discussing. [Interview: 1, 3]
- Topic demand ranking: measure how relevant or popular a topic is, e.g. how many
  people ask similar questions. [Interview: 3]
- Hooks, post ideas and first drafts: turn research into engagement hooks, post
  recommendations and draft content for LinkedIn, blogs and social. [Interview: 1, 3, 4]
- Content calendar feeding: supply an ongoing, auto-updating content calendar with
  recommended topics and posts. [Interview: 1]
- Creative briefs and tone-of-voice guides: generate reusable briefs that capture the
  audience's tone, messaging and keywords. [Interview: 4]
- Brand-voice-consistent output: apply a brand's tone, preferred words and banned words
  to generated content. [Interview: 3]
- Context packs for AI tools: export research as context for other AI tools (Claude,
  Notion, etc.) so all generated work uses the same language. [Interview: 1, 3, 4]
- Content format discovery: learn which formats the audience prefers (video, articles,
  forums, reports). [Interview: 1]
- Newsjacking and timing: spot current events and seasonal moments (e.g. Q4 budget
  cycles) to time content. [Interview: 1]
- Content curation: aggregate industry news for newsletters, roundups and social posts.
- Original data studies: gather datasets for reports, infographics and linkable
  "state of the industry" content.
- Content briefs from top-ranking pages: extract headings and subtopics from
  high-performing articles to build outlines.
- Content inventory and audits: crawl your own site to catalog pages, authors, dates
  and word counts for refreshing or pruning.
- Content refresh triggers: detect outdated stats, dead links or old dates in your
  existing articles.
- Repurposing: pull published content into structured formats for reuse in other
  channels.
- Q&A over research: ask follow-up questions of collected research. [Interview: 4]


3. COMPETITIVE INTELLIGENCE
---------------------------
- Competitor content tracking: monitor what competitors publish, how often and on
  which topics. [Interview: 3]
- Competitor and existing-solution scanning: identify who already serves the market
  and how well. [Interview: 1, 4]
- Pricing monitoring: track competitor prices, discounts, bundles and promotions.
- Product and feature tracking: catch new launches, changed specs and retired products.
- Messaging and positioning analysis: collect competitors' headlines, value
  propositions and CTAs to spot shifts.
- Website change detection: get alerts when competitors edit key pages.
- Ad intelligence: gather competitor ads from public ad libraries.
- Hiring signals: read job postings to infer competitor strategy and expansion.
- Partnership and integration tracking: watch integration directories and partner pages.
- Promotional calendar mapping: log competitors' sale timing and seasonal campaigns.
- Stock and availability tracking: spot competitor stock-outs to adjust ads.


4. SEO AND AI SEARCH VISIBILITY
-------------------------------
- AI search visibility and citations: track how often your brand and competitors are
  cited in AI answers and authoritative sources on a topic. [Interview: 3, 4]
- Visibility-driven topic selection: combine scraped signals with search and ads data
  (e.g. Search Console, Google Ads) to choose topics that raise visibility.
  [Interview: 3]
- SERP tracking: record rankings, featured snippets, "People also ask" boxes and AI
  overview citations.
- Keyword research: pull autocomplete suggestions, related searches and question lists.
- Content gap analysis: compare what competitors rank for against your coverage.
- Technical site audits: crawl your site for broken links, missing metadata, duplicate
  titles, redirect chains and orphan pages.
- Backlink prospecting: find resource pages, broken links, guest-post opportunities
  and unlinked brand mentions.
- Local SEO: monitor business listings, map results and name/address/phone consistency.
- Structured data checks: verify schema markup at scale.


5. BRAND MONITORING AND REPUTATION
----------------------------------
- Brand awareness and recognition: measure how well-known a brand or sub-brand is in a
  market. [Interview: 2]
- Brand differentiation perception: check whether people distinguish a sub-brand from
  its parent brand. [Interview: 2]
- Sentiment analysis: analyze how people feel about a brand in reviews, forums and
  social posts. [Interview: 2]
- Brand mentions: track where the brand, executives or products appear online.
- Review monitoring: aggregate reviews from marketplaces, app stores and review sites.
- Crisis detection: get early warnings when negative mentions spike.
- Brand protection: find counterfeit listings, unauthorized resellers, logo misuse and
  copied content.
- MAP compliance: check that retailers respect minimum advertised pricing.


6. CHANNELS AND SOCIAL MEDIA
----------------------------
- Channel discovery: identify where the audience spends time and where to build
  presence (e.g. Reddit communities, niche forums, platforms). [Interview: 1, 3, 4]
- LinkedIn signal tracking: monitor LinkedIn conversations and signals, a known gap
  in many tools. [Interview: 3]
- Hashtag and trend monitoring: track volume and themes around hashtags and topics.
- Influencer discovery: find creators by niche, audience size and engagement.
- Engagement benchmarking: compare your social performance with competitors'.
- User-generated content discovery: find customer posts about your brand to reshare
  with permission.
- Comment analysis: understand audience reactions to campaigns.


7. LEAD GENERATION AND GROWTH
-----------------------------
- Prospect lists: build company lists from directories, exhibitor lists and member pages.
- Account enrichment: add firmographic data (industry, size, locations) to CRM records.
- Technographic data: detect which tools a company's website uses for ABM targeting.
- Event mining: collect speakers and sponsors from industry events for outreach.
- Intent signals: track funding news, hiring and expansions that signal buying intent.


8. E-COMMERCE AND PRODUCT MARKETING
-----------------------------------
- Catalog enrichment: gather specs, descriptions and images from suppliers and partners.
- Marketplace listing optimization: analyze top sellers' titles, bullets and keywords.


9. PERFORMANCE MARKETING AND ANALYTICS
--------------------------------------
- Landing page monitoring: confirm your pages are live, correct and match ad copy.
- Affiliate and partner compliance: check affiliates use correct links, disclosures
  and messaging.
- Ad placement verification: confirm where and how your ads appear.
- Dashboard feeds: pull data from sources without an API into reporting dashboards.


10. PR AND COMMUNICATIONS
-------------------------
- Media list building: find journalists covering specific beats.
- Coverage tracking: compile press mentions for reporting.
- Award and speaking opportunities: collect calls for entries and speakers.


11. INTERNAL OPERATIONS
-----------------------
- Website QA: check translations, images and pricing consistency across regional sites.
- Content governance: verify disclaimers, cookie banners and brand guidelines across
  microsites.
- CMS migration: extract content from an old CMS when moving to a new one.
- Knowledge bases for AI: collect clean source material for internal chatbots and
  AI-assisted writing.
